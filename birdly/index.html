Birdly

Your pocket guide to the birds around you. Learn names through short games and repetition — garden visitors, seasonal migrants, and raptors.

Android uses the same lessons and the same design. Progress stays on each device.

Screenshots

What it does

  • Discover topics

    Four sets of ten birds: common garden species, winter visitors, summer migrants, and raptors. Each bird has perched and in-flight photos.

  • Play five games

    Start with an introduction, then multiple choice, true or false, letter selection, and word search as you get more confident.

  • Keep a bird log

    See every bird you have practised and how well you know it, grouped by topic.

  • Train in the Dojo

    Once a bird has been introduced, mix them all together for extra practice.

How you learn

Games get harder as your mastery goes up. Wrong answers bring a bird back into practice.

  1. 1

    Introduction

    See the bird, its name, and a short description before anything is tested.

  2. 2

    Multiple choice and true or false

    Pick the right name, or decide whether the photo matches the label.

  3. 3

    Spell it, then find it

    Build the name letter by letter, then hunt for it in a word search.

Support

Answers to common questions. If you still need help, get in touch below.

Does Birdly need an internet connection?
No. Photos, names, and games are stored in the app. You can learn fully offline.
Do I need an account?
No. There are no accounts, ads, or analytics. See the privacy policy.
Where is my progress stored?
On that phone or tablet. iPhone and Android copies do not sync with each other. Deleting the app removes the local progress.
How do I turn sounds or haptics off?
Open Settings from Discover. You can toggle sounds and haptic feedback independently.
Is the source code available?
Yes. Birdly is free software under the GNU GPL v3. iPhone: github.com/anthonyc-r/birdly. Android: github.com/anthonyc-r/birdly-android.
